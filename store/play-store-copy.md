## Full description (≤4000 chars)

```
Western scales divide sound into 12 equal parts. Turkish makam uses 53 — creating intervals that exist nowhere on a piano. Modal jazz found seven feeling-worlds hiding inside one scale. The Modality teaches you to hear both — and the ancient connections between them.

TWO TRADITIONS, ONE EAR

• Turkish Makam — 29 makams from Rast to Ferahfeza, each with its scale drawn degree by degree (with the microtonal commas marked), its seyir, its mood, its time of day, and the ney to play it.
• Modal Jazz — 14 modes across two scales: the seven modes of the major scale, and the seven modes of melodic minor — the language of tension, from Lydian dominant to the altered scale.

THE CROSSROADS

The feature no other app has: where the traditions touch. Hicaz and the Phrygian dominant share one ancient interval — hear them side by side, ney against Rhodes piano. Saba and Locrian reach grief by the same trick. The comparison player puts two civilizations one tap apart.

THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE MUSIC

64 musicians, from Itri and Dede Efendi to Miles Davis and Bill Evans — with portraits, lives, and every place in the app you can hear them. Tap any name on any recording and meet the person.

HEAR IT EVERYWHERE

• Iconic recordings and taksim improvisations for every major makam
• Canonical tunes for every mode — So What, Maiden Voyage, Spain
• Songs you already know, mapped to their makam — from classical to arabesk to Anatolian rock
• Play any scale on the ney or Rhodes, from any root

LEARN IT PROPERLY

• A guided lesson path in each tradition, from "What is a makam?" up
• Quizzes that unlock level by level
• A glossary of both traditions' vocabulary — taksim, seyir, voicing, vamp
• Usul rhythm patterns with audio

Built for curious listeners, Western musicians meeting microtones for the first time, and Turkish speakers rediscovering their own tradition.

One tradition rearranges the twelve notes; the other reaches past them. Neither is more advanced. Come hear why.
```

## Notes

  and full description, so keep the strongest terms (makam, maqam, jazz modes,
  music theory, ney) in the first lines.
- Short description is the most ASO-weighted text — it leads with "Turkish
  makams & jazz modes."
